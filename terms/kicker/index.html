What is a kicker in poker

How the kicker decides between equal hands, when it does not play and the pot is split. Two interactive examples.

All termsTake a seat
Cards and hands
A kicker is the highest card that is not part of the hand itself but decides the winner when players hold the same hand. With a pair of aces each, the player with the higher next card wins.

Example: same pair, different kicker

When the kicker does not play

A kicker counts only if it is part of the best five cards. If the board cards are higher than your extra cards, kickers do not play and the pot is split:

Both best hands are AAKQJ. The three and the two do not make it in, so the pot is split.

Where kickers matter most

  • A pair with one hole card. AK against AQ on an ace-high flop is the classic case.
  • Trips with a paired board. On KK852, K9 beats K7: the trips are the same, the kicker decides.
  • High card. If nobody has a hand, cards are compared one by one from the highest down.

Kicker trouble

Beginners overrate hands like A8 or K10: they make a pair of aces or kings and lose to a better kicker. That is why such hands are best folded from early position. More on positions on the No-Limit Hold’em page.

Kickers in Omaha

In Omaha a hand is exactly two hole cards plus three board cards, so the kicker also comes only from those five. Kicker ties are rarer in Omaha: stronger hands usually decide.
