What is a draw in poker: flush draws and straight draws

The types of draws, their outs and odds, what combo and backdoor draws are, and how to play draws. With an outs calculator.

All termsTake a seat
Cards and hands
A draw is an unfinished hand: you need one more card to make a straight or a flush. Four cards of one suit are a flush draw; four cards in a row are a straight draw. The cards that complete it are your outs.

Nut and non-nut draws

Not every draw is worth the same. A flush draw with the ace of the suit is a nut draw: if it hits, it is the best hand. A low flush draw can hit and still lose to a higher flush. Straight draws work the same way: drawing to the low end of a straight risks losing to a higher one. More in the nuts.

How to play draws

  • Compare your odds with the price. Calling 10 into a pot of 30 makes a final pot of 40, of which you put in 25%. The call pays if your chance to hit is at least that share.
  • Play draws aggressively. A bet or raise with a draw is a semi-bluff: the opponent may fold now, and if they call, you still have outs.
  • Mind the turn. From the flop to the river your chance is almost twice the one-card chance. A big turn bet often makes the draw unprofitable.
  • Draws are stronger in Omaha. With four to six hole cards you often have 13–20 outs. But the 2 + 3 rule still applies: a flush needs exactly two suited hole cards.
