Flop, turn and river in poker: the order of streets

What the streets in poker are called, how many cards each one shows, who acts first and what a showdown is. With an interactive hand.

All termsTake a seat
Cards and hands
A hand of Hold’em or Omaha is played in streets: preflop — hole cards only, the flop — three community cards, the turn — the fourth, the river — the fifth. Each street has a betting round, and after the river the remaining players show their cards. That is the showdown.

The order of streets

StreetWhat is dealtWho acts first
PreflopHole cards: 2 in Hold’em, 4–6 in OmahaThe player left of the big blind
FlopThree community cards at onceThe first active player left of the button
TurnThe fourth community cardThe first active player left of the button
RiverThe fifth community cardThe first active player left of the button
ShowdownPlayers’ cardsThe last player to bet on the river; if nobody bet, the first in order

Before every street the dealer “burns” — sets aside — the top card of the deck. Online the software does it, but the rule is the same.

How the game changes street by street

  • The flop is the key street: you see five of seven cards and know whether your hand connected.
  • The turn is more expensive: bets usually grow and a draw has only one card left.
  • The river is the last chance to bet. Draws have hit or missed, which shapes both bluffs and value bets.
