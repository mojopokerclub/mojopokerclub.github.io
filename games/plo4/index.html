Omaha PLO4: rules, hands and how it differs from Hold’em

Four-card Pot-Limit Omaha — the key “2 + 3” rule, how pot-limit is counted, typical beginner mistakes and playing Omaha in the club.

How to play

Pot-Limit Omaha (PLO, PLO4) is the second most popular poker game after Hold’em. The hand flows the same way — blinds, preflop, flop, turn, river — with two differences that change everything: you get four hole cards and must make your hand from exactly two of them and exactly three board cards.

The key Omaha rule: 2 + 3

This is what trips up Hold’em players most. You cannot play one hole card with four from the board, and you cannot use three of your own.

Actions at the table

The actions in PLO4 are the same as in Hold’em. The one difference: you can bet or raise at most the size of the pot.

CheckPass without betting. Only possible if nobody has bet in this round.
BetThe first bet in a betting round.
CallMatch the opponent’s current bet.
RaiseIncrease the opponent’s bet. A re-raise is a 3-bet, the next one a 4-bet.
FoldThrow your cards away and leave the hand.
All-inPut all your chips in. If the opponent has more, the excess goes to a side pot.

How pot-limit works

The maximum bet is the size of the pot. To raise, first count your call, then add the resulting pot. Example: pot 10, opponent bets 10. Your maximum raise is 10 (call) + 30 (pot after the call) = 40. The “Pot” button in ClubGG does this for you.

Hands in Omaha

The rankings are the same as in Hold’em, but strong hands come up far more often: four hole cards make 6 different two-card combinations, so straights, flushes and full houses at showdown are routine. Two pair and top pair, strong in Hold’em, often lose in Omaha.

Positions at the table

Strong PLO4 starting hands

  • Double-suited — two suits in pairs, e.g. AKJ10: two different flush draws.
  • Rundowns — connected cards like J1098: many straight possibilities.
  • Supported pairs — AAKQ suited; a bare pair of aces is much weaker than in Hold’em.
  • Avoid “danglers” — one card unrelated to the other three.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Playing a flush with one suited card — it does not exist in Omaha.
  • Going all-in with a non-nut flush or straight: in Omaha the nuts — the best possible hand on that board — matter.
  • Overvaluing an unsupported pair of aces.
  • Playing too many hands from early position: position matters even more than in Hold’em.

Omaha terms

2 + 3 rule
Your hand uses exactly two hole cards and three board cards.
Double-suited
Two pairs of suited cards — two different flush draws.
Rundown
Connected cards (J-10-9-8) — many straight possibilities.
Dangler
A card unrelated to the rest of the hand; weakens it.
Nuts
The best possible hand on the board. In Omaha the nuts usually win.
Blocker
A card that stops an opponent from holding a hand — e.g. the ace of a suit against the nut flush.
Pot
The button for a pot-sized bet.

Table settings in the club

Every table in the lobby carries tags. Here is what they mean:

SettingWhat it means
AnteA small forced bet from every player before the deal. Pots are bigger from the start, so playing more hands pays off.
Bomb PotA special hand: everyone posts the same amount, preflop is skipped and the flop is dealt straight away. Usually every N hands — adds a lot of action.
Double BoardTwo boards are dealt at once and the pot is split: half to the best hand on each board. You can win one board and save half the pot.
Run it Multi-TimesWhen players are all-in before the river, the remaining cards can be dealt two or three times and the pot split by result. Lower variance, fewer bad beats.
VPIP requirementThe table requires each player to voluntarily put chips in a minimum share of hands, so nobody just sits and waits.
GPS & IP restrictionPlayers on the same IP or located close to each other cannot sit at one table — protection against collusion. The private MOJO : ALLIN club has no such restriction.
10BB / 20BB / 50BB / 60BB MaxTables with a capped buy-in — e.g. at most 20 big blinds. Short-stack play: more all-ins preflop and on the flop, simpler and faster decisions.
