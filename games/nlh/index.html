How to play

No-Limit Hold’em (NLH) is Texas Hold’em with no cap on bets — the most popular poker game in the world. Each player gets two hole cards, five community cards are dealt, and the best five-card hand made from those seven wins. “No-Limit” means you can bet your whole stack at any time — all-in.

History of Texas Hold’em

Hold’em appeared in early 20th-century Texas — the town of Robstown is officially recognised as its birthplace. Texan players brought it to Las Vegas in 1967, and since 1971 the World Series of Poker Main Event has been played as No-Limit Hold’em. The real boom came in 2003, when amateur Chris Moneymaker won the Main Event after qualifying through an online satellite. Since then NLH has been the standard both live and online.

Limit, Pot-Limit and No-Limit Hold’em

The hand plays the same way; only the bet size differs:

StructureHow much you can betNotes
Limit (FL)Fixed bets: a small step preflop and on the flop, double on the turn and riverSlow, mathematical, rarely played today
Pot-Limit (PL)Up to the current size of the potThe standard for Omaha
No-Limit (NL)Any amount up to your whole stack (all-in)The most popular format — the one played in the club

The goal

Win the pot: either hold the best five-card hand from your two cards and the five on board at showdown, or make every opponent fold earlier. A standard 52-card deck is used, with 2 to 9 players at a table.

How a hand plays

  1. Blinds. The two players left of the button post forced bets: small blind (SB) and big blind (BB) — e.g. 0.10 and 0.20 chips at 0.10/0.20.
  2. Preflop. Everyone gets two cards. Action starts left of the big blind: fold, call or raise.
  3. Flop. Three community cards and the second betting round; the first active player left of the button acts first.
  4. Turn. The fourth card and the third betting round.
  5. River. The fifth card and the final betting round.
  6. Showdown. Remaining players show; the best five-card hand wins. You may use both, one or none of your hole cards.

Position matters most

The button acts last on every street after the flop and sees opponents’ decisions first. From late positions (button, cutoff) you can play far more hands; from early ones (UTG) only strong hands. This one rule moves a beginner’s results more than anything else.

Texas Hold’em terms

Hole cards
The two face-down cards each player receives.
Board
The community cards in the middle — flop, turn and river.
Kicker
The highest card outside the combination; breaks ties.
Nuts
The best possible hand on the current board.
Draw
An unfinished hand one card short of a straight or flush.
Set
Three of a kind made with a pocket pair and one board card.
Limp
Entering the pot by just calling the big blind.
Straddle
A voluntary blind bet of two big blinds before the deal.
Side pot
A separate pot for the bigger stacks when someone is all-in for less.

Starter tips

  • Start tight: pairs 77+, AK–AT, KQ, suited aces and connectors from late position.
  • Enter pots with a raise, not a limp — take the initiative.
  • Bet the flop when it suits your hand or scares your opponent, not on every board.
  • Watch stack depth: 10BB/20BB Max tables are about all-ins, 100 BB play is about postflop decisions.
  • Do not chase losses after a big pot — take a break.

Table settings in the club

Every table in the lobby carries tags. Here is what they mean:

SettingWhat it means
AnteA small forced bet from every player before the deal. Pots are bigger from the start, so playing more hands pays off.
Bomb PotA special hand: everyone posts the same amount, preflop is skipped and the flop is dealt straight away. Usually every N hands — adds a lot of action.
Double BoardTwo boards are dealt at once and the pot is split: half to the best hand on each board. You can win one board and save half the pot.
Run it Multi-TimesWhen players are all-in before the river, the remaining cards can be dealt two or three times and the pot split by result. Lower variance, fewer bad beats.
VPIP requirementThe table requires each player to voluntarily put chips in a minimum share of hands, so nobody just sits and waits.
GPS & IP restrictionPlayers on the same IP or located close to each other cannot sit at one table — protection against collusion. The private MOJO : ALLIN club has no such restriction.
10BB / 20BB / 50BB / 60BB MaxTables with a capped buy-in — e.g. at most 20 big blinds. Short-stack play: more all-ins preflop and on the flop, simpler and faster decisions.
