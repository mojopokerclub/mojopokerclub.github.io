PLO6 (Six-Card Omaha): rules and features

Six-card Pot-Limit Omaha — the most action-packed Omaha. Rules, 15 combinations per hand, why the nuts win here and how PLO6 tables work in the club.

Take a seat How to join

How to play

PLO6 (Six-Card Omaha) gives everyone six hole cards. The rule is the same as in PLO4 and PLO5: exactly two hole cards and three board cards. Six cards make 15 different pairs, so almost everyone has a draw or a made hand on the flop — the most action-packed Omaha there is.

How the game plays

  • The nuts are everything. With six cards someone almost always has the best hand; a second-best flush is often a fold.
  • Little bluffing, lots of value. Opponents have something to call with — bet strong hands, check more without them.
  • Blockers matter. Holding the ace of a suit means nobody else has the nut flush in it — useful on the river.
  • Run it Multi-Times. All-ins in PLO6 are often close (55/45), so running the board two or three times is sensible variance insurance.
  • Double Board. PLO6 tables with two boards let you win half the pot even if you lose one board.

Which hands to play

A good PLO6 hand is one where most of the six cards work together: two suits of two or three cards, connected ranks, nut potential (the ace of a suit, top straight cards). A strong example: AKQJ109 — three suits, a long rundown, nut flush draws. Weak hands are scattered, unsuited, with gaps.

Positions at the table

Your position decides when you act. The later you act, the more you know about your opponents’ decisions. In Omaha this matters even more: pots grow fast and a decision without information costs more. Tap a seat to read about it.

How pot-limit is counted

The maximum bet equals the pot. For a maximum raise, first call the opponent’s bet, then add the whole resulting pot. Try your own numbers:

Omaha terms

2 + 3 rule
Your hand uses exactly two hole cards and three board cards.
Double-suited
Two pairs of suited cards — two different flush draws.
Rundown
Connected cards (J-10-9-8) — many straight possibilities.
Dangler
A card unrelated to the rest of the hand; weakens it.
Nuts
The best possible hand on the board. In Omaha the nuts usually win.
Blocker
A card that stops an opponent from holding a hand — e.g. the ace of a suit against the nut flush.
Pot
The button for a pot-sized bet.

Table settings in the club

Every table in the lobby carries tags. Here is what they mean:

SettingWhat it means
AnteA small forced bet from every player before the deal. Pots are bigger from the start, so playing more hands pays off.
Double BoardTwo boards are dealt at once and the pot is split: half to the best hand on each board. You can win one board and save half the pot.
Run it Multi-TimesWhen players are all-in before the river, the remaining cards can be dealt two or three times and the pot split by result. Lower variance, fewer bad beats.
VPIP requirementThe table requires each player to voluntarily put chips in a minimum share of hands, so nobody just sits and waits.
GPS & IP restrictionPlayers on the same IP or located close to each other cannot sit at one table — protection against collusion. The private MOJO : ALLIN club has no such restriction.
10BB / 20BB / 50BB / 60BB MaxTables with a capped buy-in — e.g. at most 20 big blinds. Short-stack play: more all-ins preflop and on the flop, simpler and faster decisions.
