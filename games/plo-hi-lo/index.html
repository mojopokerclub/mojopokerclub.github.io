Omaha Hi-Lo: rules, the low hand and worked examples

How the pot splits in Omaha Hi-Lo, what a low and “eight or better” mean, when there is no low, and what scooping, quartering and counterfeiting are. Worked hands and how PLO5 Hi-Lo (Big O) differs from PLO6 Hi-Lo.

How to play

Omaha Hi-Lo (Hi/Lo, “eight or better”, O8) is Omaha with a split pot. At showdown the pot is divided in two. One half goes to the best high hand, as in regular Omaha. The other half goes to the best low hand. If nobody makes a low, the high hand takes the whole pot.

MOJO : MU runs two versions. PLO5 Hi-Lo, also called Big O, deals five hole cards. PLO6 Hi-Lo deals six. The split-pot rules are the same; the number of cards and which hands are strong are what change.

The rules in a minute

  1. Each player gets 5 cards in PLO5 Hi-Lo or 6 in PLO6 Hi-Lo. Betting is pot-limit: you can bet at most the size of the pot.
  2. Betting follows the Hold’em streets: preflop, the flop with three community cards, the turn and the river.
  3. At showdown everyone makes two hands at once, a high and a low. Each uses exactly two hole cards and exactly three board cards.
  4. The best high takes half the pot and the best low takes the other half.
  5. A low needs five different cards, eight or lower. If nobody has one, the high hand gets the whole pot.
  6. Tied hands share their half equally.

What a low hand is

  • A low is five cards of different ranks, all eight or lower. The ace counts as one for low and stays high for high.
  • Straights and flushes do not hurt a low. A2345 is both a straight for high and the best possible low.
  • Pairs ruin a low. AA or 22 in your hand cannot make one; you need two different low cards.
  • Lows are compared from the top card down, and the lower top card wins. 7542A beats 8432A because seven is lower than eight.
  • If the top cards tie, compare the next ones. 76432 beats 7652A: on the third card, four is lower than five.

A low is named after its top card: an eight-low, a seven-low, a six-low. The lower the top card, the stronger the hand, and nothing beats the wheel.

The 2 + 3 rule applies to both halves

The key difference from regular Omaha: you pick cards for high and for low independently. Each is exactly two hole cards and three board cards, but the pairs can differ. With A2KKQ on a 378KJ board, A2 makes the low 8732A. KK makes the high, a set of kings.

A common mistake: the low on the board

Beginners often read only the board. Even with A2345 on the table you may not have the wheel. You still need exactly two hole cards, both low and of different ranks from the three board cards in your low. With KKQJ9 on that board you have no low at all. With 67KQJ your best low is 7632A: the six and seven from your hand plus A23 from the board.

When there is no low

A low is only possible if the board has at least three different ranks from ace to eight. With fewer, nobody can make a low and the best high hand takes the whole pot.

A2 is the best pair of cards for low, and the kings give you chances for high. But there is no third low card.

On the 348 flop the A2 holds the nut low, 8432A; nothing better is possible on that board. The 2 on the turn changes everything. Your low stays the same, but now an opponent with A5 has the wheel, and A6, or a five with a six, beats you too. A board card matched your low card and devalued it. This is called counterfeiting.

The protection is a third low card in your hand. With a five instead of one of the kings, you would have the wheel on that turn: A5 from your hand and 234 from the board. That is why hands with three low cards, such as A24 or A25, are worth more than a bare A2.

PLO5 Hi-Lo (Big O) and PLO6 Hi-Lo

Both versions run in MOJO : MU with the same low and split rules. But the more cards each player holds, the more often opponents have both a strong high and a good low. In PLO6 Hi-Lo you need the nuts more often and split halves more often.

PLO5 Hi-Lo, or Big O

Many rooms and clubs call five-card Omaha Hi-Lo Big O. A strong Big O hand holds A2 or A3 with another low card and a suited ace, for example A24KQ. It has two ways to make a low, a nut flush draw and high cards for the high half.

PLO6 Hi-Lo

With six cards almost everyone has a decent hand, so second-best lows and middling flushes often lose. Play hands with A2, a backup low card and nut potential for high, such as A235KQ. Scattered hands with one or two low cards are usually a fold.

Which hands to play

Best
A-2 with one or two more low cards and a suited ace. These play both ways and are protected against counterfeiting.
Strong
A-3 with low backup and high cards, and aces with A-2.
Speculative
Low rundowns without an ace. They draw to the wheel, but the low is often second-best and the high is weak.
Weak
Middle cards without an ace. They make no low and rarely the nut high.
Trap
A bare A-2 with no third low card and no suit. It is easily counterfeited or quartered.
A strong PLO6 Hi-Lo hand
A-2 with a backup three and five, a suited ace and high cards for the high half.

Starter tips

  • Play to scoop. A hand that can only win half the pot rarely justifies a big investment.
  • Aim for the nut low. In Hi-Lo the second-best low often loses or gets split.
  • Read the board. With two or more high cards on the flop a low may never come, and the high decides everything.
  • Once a low is impossible, play it like regular Omaha: the whole pot goes to the high hand.
  • Be careful re-raising with only a low. The pot is split and you risk getting a quarter.
  • New to Omaha? Learn PLO4 first, then add the low.
