How to play

A multi-table tournament (MTT) runs several tables at once. Everyone starts with the same stack, blinds rise by levels, busted players free up seats and tables merge — down to the final table and a winner. Unlike cash games, tournament chips are not exchanged: you play for a finishing place.

How a tournament works

  1. Registration. Register in the tournament lobby. Many events allow late registration during the first levels.
  2. Start. Everyone gets the same starting stack — e.g. 10,000 chips.
  3. Blind levels. Blinds rise every few minutes, an ante kicks in at some level. Stacks shrink relative to the blinds and play speeds up.
  4. The bubble. Just before the paid places: the next player out gets nothing. Play is most cautious here.
  5. In the money and the final table. After the bubble everyone left is rewarded; the final table decides the top places.

Strategy by stage

Early stage (deep stacks)

Your stack is big relative to the blinds, so you can play more hands in position and outplay postflop. Do not risk your stack without a strong hand — surviving matters more than chipping up fast.

Middle stage (20–40 BB)

Antes appear and preflop pots get more valuable. Open more from late position, steal blinds and use resteals (all-in re-raises) against frequent openers.

Bubble and short stack (under 15 BB)

Decisions become shove-or-fold. Big stacks pressure the medium ones, medium stacks avoid risky calls. Short-stacked, take the first good spot to shove — waiting too long costs more.

Final table

Every next place is worth noticeably more. Watch the stacks: avoid needless clashes with the chip leader and pressure those afraid to bust.

Tournament terms

Buy-in
The cost of entering a tournament. Freerolls are free.
Starting stack
The number of tournament chips each player starts with.
Blind level
A time period with fixed blinds; blinds rise after it.
Late registration
Entering a tournament after it starts, during the first levels.
Rebuy / add-on
Buying more chips: a rebuy after busting or when short, an add-on once at the end of the rebuy period.
Bounty
A reward for knocking out a specific player.
Bubble
The last place before the paid places.
ITM (In The Money)
The stage when everyone left is paid.
ICM
A model valuing a tournament stack by the payouts; key on the bubble and at the final table.
