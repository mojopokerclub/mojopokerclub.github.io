Poker Rakeback Explained: What It Is and How to Calculate It

In plain language: what rake and rakeback are, which return schemes exist and how much it adds up to at each stake — with a calculator.

Rakeback is the return of part of the rake — the fee a poker room or club takes from every pot or tournament entry — back to the player. It is expressed as a percentage of the rake paid: with 30% rakeback a player who paid 1,000 chips of rake in a month gets 300 back.

What rake is and where it comes from

In cash games rake is usually 3–5% of the pot with a cap of a few big blinds; most rooms take it only when the hand reaches the flop (“no flop, no drop”). In tournaments rake is the entry fee, e.g. 100 + 10, where the 10 is the fee. Rake is the main source of income for any poker platform.

How rakeback is calculated

The formula is simple: rakeback = rake paid × rakeback percentage. What matters is how the rake is attributed: with the *dealt* method rake is split between everyone dealt in; with *contributed* only between players who put chips in the pot, in proportion to their contribution; *weighted contributed* does the same more precisely. The same percentage yields different amounts under different methods.

Types of rakeback schemes

  • Flat percentage — the most transparent: the rate does not depend on volume and is known upfront.
  • Tiered loyalty programs — the rate grows with points or status; the more you play, the higher the return.
  • Wheels, missions and bonuses — part of the rake comes back as random prizes; the final rate is unknown in advance.
  • Agent rakeback — in club poker the return terms are agreed individually with the agent or club manager.

What to compare

  1. The percentage — together with the rake attribution method (dealt / contributed).
  2. The rake itself and the cap: 40% of a high rake can be worse than 25% of a low one.
  3. Payout frequency — daily, weekly or monthly.
  4. Conditions: minimum volume, validity period, limits on stakes and formats.
